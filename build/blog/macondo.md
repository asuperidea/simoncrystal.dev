# Macondo Hackathon
## From August 14-16, I attended a Hack Club hackathon in Bogotá, Colombia.
Hackathon! This was the word that drew me to Hack Club's Macondo program in the first place. I really wanted to go. Even though I had never been to a hackathon and never really knew about them, I was determined to go.
I had the opportunity to spend the 13th in Bogotá, experiencing the amazing vibe of the city. I tried amazing foods and also got to work on this very website.

The hackathon itself was a blast and a blur. It started rough when I got teamed up with two Spanish speakers; we agreed that it would be best if I joined a new team. My new team also made me unsure of myself. They were both older and more experienced than me. They also wanted to develop a game, something I didn't really know much about. After some brainstorming, I felt more prepared but went to bed nervous.

Day 2 was a real turning point. We made excellent progress on our game and bonded more (I mean, it's hard not to when you're spending hours in a cubicle). I felt a lot more useful; I was understanding Unity more and feeling more confident in myself. Even though the progress we made was great, the game still needed much more development before it could become what we really wanted.

Later in the day, we went to Monserrate, a beautiful mountain that overlooks all of Bogotá. We took cable cars up and a funicular down.

Day 3, the final day, was really stressful. We were up late into the early morning, so we slept in. We began work at around 9 AM, with only two and a half hours until projects were due. Every change sparked a billion more issues that we had to clean up. In the end, our game worked, but it was lacking a lot of features we wanted.

My teammates had to leave very early to catch their flight, leaving me with an issue: the game had a bug. Essentially, the button to start the minigame didn't call the correct function. Through some help from the amazing organizers and my teammates on Slack, I got the game working in time for the presentation!

Although we didn't win any awards, the environment, experience, and project gave me a new love for hackathons.
